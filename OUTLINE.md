# CAD/CAM in Dentistry: From Scan to Restoration
## Book Outline (Draft 1)

Each chapter follows the template in `STYLE.md`: learning objectives, numbered sections, clinical and laboratory boxes, key points, review questions and references.

---

## Part I — Foundations

### Chapter 1. Introduction to CAD/CAM in Dentistry *(drafted)*
Definitions; history (Duret, Mörmann, the CEREC system); the three parts of every system (acquisition, design, manufacture); subtractive and additive manufacturing; chairside, laboratory and centralised production; open and closed systems; overview of materials; summary of the evidence; advantages and limitations.

### Chapter 2. The Digital Workflow and Data Formats *(drafted)*
- Analogue, hybrid and fully digital workflows
- File formats: STL, PLY, OBJ, DICOM, 3MF; meshes, point clouds and colour/texture data
- Open and closed architectures; interoperability; vendor lock-in
- Data transfer, cloud platforms, storage, backup
- Data protection and patient privacy (GDPR, HIPAA principles)
- The "virtual patient": merging IOS, CBCT, facial scans and jaw motion

### Chapter 3. Accuracy in Digital Dentistry: Concepts and Measurement *(drafted)*
- Trueness and precision (ISO 5725 and ISO 12836)
- Reference scanners, best-fit alignment and colour-map analysis
- Marginal and internal fit: definitions, measurement methods, clinically acceptable ranges
- How error accumulates from scan to final restoration
- Reading accuracy studies critically: in vitro versus in vivo

## Part II — Data Acquisition

### Chapter 4. Intraoral Scanners *(drafted)*
- Optical principles: confocal microscopy, active triangulation, active wavefront sampling, structured light, OCT
- Hardware features: tip size, powder-free scanning, colour, heat, wireless
- Scanning strategies and paths; stitching and tracking
- Accuracy for single units, short spans, complete arches and implants
- Soft-tissue management, margins, bleeding and subgingival preparations
- Bite registration and virtual articulation
- Learning curve, ergonomics, infection control
- Patient-reported outcomes; time efficiency

### Chapter 5. Laboratory (Extraoral) Scanners and Model Digitisation *(drafted)*
- Structured-light and laser desktop scanners
- Scanning impressions versus casts
- Articulator transfer and digital mounting
- Calibration and maintenance

### Chapter 6. Three-Dimensional Imaging Beyond the Teeth *(drafted)*
- CBCT for CAD/CAM: segmentation and registration with surface scans
- Facial scanning and photogrammetry
- Jaw-tracking and electronic axiographs
- Digital smile design and the 3D facially driven workflow

## Part III — Computer-Aided Design

### Chapter 7. Design Software: Principles and Tools *(drafted)*
- Libraries, biogeneric and anatomic proposals
- Margin detection, insertion axis, undercuts and blockout
- Parameters: cement space, minimum thickness, connector dimensions
- Occlusal design: static and dynamic contacts, virtual articulators
- Artificial intelligence in automatic design

### Chapter 8. Designing Tooth-Supported Restorations
- Inlays, onlays, overlays, endocrowns
- Veneers and additive mock-ups
- Single crowns and multi-unit FDPs
- Preparation guidelines for CAD/CAM materials

### Chapter 9. Designing Implant Restorations
- Scan bodies, libraries and digital implant analogues
- Stock versus custom abutments; Ti-base (hybrid) abutments
- Screw-retained and cement-retained designs; angled screw channels
- Complete-arch implant frameworks; passive fit

## Part IV — Computer-Aided Manufacturing

### Chapter 10. Subtractive Manufacturing: Milling and Grinding
- Machine axes (3, 4, 5); wet and dry milling
- Tools, burs, toolpath strategies and CAM software
- Milling resolution, minimum radius and edge chipping
- Chairside mills versus laboratory and industrial centres
- Pre-sintered and fully sintered materials; sintering and crystallisation furnaces

### Chapter 11. Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing)
- Technologies: SLA, DLP, LCD/MSLA, material jetting, binder jetting, FDM, SLM/DMLS
- Printing parameters: layer thickness, orientation, supports, post-curing
- Dental applications: models, surgical guides, splints, trays, provisional and definitive resins, denture bases and teeth, metal frameworks
- Biocompatibility and regulation of printable resins
- Ceramic and zirconia printing: current status

### Chapter 12. Finishing, Characterisation and Quality Control
- Staining, glazing, polishing protocols
- Fit checks: digital and physical
- Troubleshooting common manufacturing failures

## Part V — Materials for CAD/CAM

### Chapter 13. Glass-Ceramics

### Chapter 14. Zirconia

- Monolithic versus veneered restorations; bonding to zirconia

### Chapter 15. Resin-Based and Hybrid Materials; Metals
- Resin nanoceramics, polymer-infiltrated ceramic networks, CAD/CAM composites
- PMMA and high-performance polymers (PEEK, PEKK)
- CAD/CAM cobalt-chromium and titanium; milled versus laser-sintered metal
