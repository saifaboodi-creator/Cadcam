## Discarded findings

1. **15.2.5 "performed poorly in several clinical trials" (C7).** Not an error. Each of the three trials listed reports worse PICN performance in some respect: survival [9], survival and wear [10], and a higher technical complication rate that ref 11's authors highlight ("the higher technical complication rate in the PICN group reported separately for this cohort should also be considered").
2. **Table 15.4 single implant crown "Lithium disilicate, ZLS or zirconia on Ti-base [9,10,12]" (A17).** The combined citation supports every element: lithium disilicate on Ti-base [9], ZLS on Ti-base [10], and lithium disilicate, silicate ceramics and zirconia for implant single crowns [12].
3. **Key Points "fewer flaws than direct or laboratory composites" (C26).** The review explicitly frames its comparison "as compared with direct and artisanal indirect composites", so the comparator is supported by 26933136.
4. **Uncited general statements (A22, B16 a–d, B16 e second part, B16 f second part, C3 repair/firing part, C22 second part, C24 FDP row, C25).** These cover repair with composite, "no firing", the typical bonding row, the denser industrially polymerised PMMA, PAEK inertness and low surface energy, aesthetic or allergy reasons for metal-free RPDs, titanium casting difficulty, the Co-Cr clinical record and veneer chipping, and the multi-unit FDP row. They are standard statements with no reference attached, no factual error was found, and several are already listed in the notes as uncited. Adding them to the notes' uncited list is an editorial choice.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- B8: the point that the Learning Objectives use "dentine-like". Learning Objective 1 does not.
- A13: the point about the 15.4.2 lead sentence ("PAEK polymers… need surface pretreatment"). It carries no citation and is a general statement; only the Key Point is confirmed (finding 33).
- A19 and C20: the point that Table 15.3 titanium "CAD/CAM milled better than cast [24]" is unsupported. Ref 24 found that material had minimal influence on fit, so the statement holds for titanium frameworks.
- A18: the point that zirconia is unsupported in the complete-arch row. Ref 24 covers CAD/CAM frameworks of several materials, and the row cross-refers to Chapter 9; only the [18] citation is confirmed (finding 32).
- B14: the point that "comparable" fit is confusing. It is the authors' own wording; only the missing in vitro label is confirmed (finding 21).
- A8 and B11: the claim that 70 and 65 µm are definitely the milling and SLM values. The abstract is ambiguous (finding 25).

## Top items for the author

1. **#5 (major): Table 15.4 RPD row cited to [21–23].** All three are in vitro crown-coping studies, not RPD studies, and two of them rank SLM below other routes, yet SLM is listed first.
2. **#3 (major): "all routes can produce clinically acceptable Co-Cr restorations".** Only one of the three in vitro studies says this; another found milling and SLM worse than casting. Fix the text, Key Points and Table 15.3 together.
3. **#4 (major): 0.6 mm rule extended to CAD/CAM composites.** Refs 7 and 8 studied only PICN, and ref 8 has no thickness data.
4. **#7 (major): Key Point on PEEK RPD retention.** It gives only ref 20's result, although ref 19 found retention similar to Co-Cr.
5. **#1 and #2 (major): ref 1 and ref 2 over-read.** "Dentine-like" modulus (five places, including Review Question 2) and "both groups… little chipping" are not in the abstracts; ref 2 supports edge stability for PICN only.
