# Chapter 11 review: Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing)

**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Method:** Three independent checker subagents (A, B, C) each checked the chapter, its 26 references and the three figures. A false-positive filter subagent then merged duplicates and re-checked every merged finding against the chapter text, the PubMed abstract and metadata of the cited paper, and the SVG source for figure findings. Evidence is limited to PubMed abstracts and metadata; full texts were not read.

## Discarded findings

1. **Uncited general statements (remaining parts of A18, B18, C23).** "Printed models have replaced stone casts in many digital workflows"; "among the most established printed devices"; "SLM … widely used"; debinding and sintering shrinkage; "other methods, such as material jetting, are also being developed"; under- and over-curing effects; "international standard terminology"; "Printed zirconia is promising for complex shapes"; "Uncured methacrylate resins are skin sensitisers". These are correct standard statements with no reference attached, and no factual error was found. The notes already list them as uncited general knowledge. Adding sources (for example ISO/ASTM 52900, an occupational-health source) is an editorial choice. "Most are ceramic-filled methacrylates" is consistent with PMID 42738164 ("ceramic-filled/methacrylate-based resins").
2. **11.3.1 Splints: sample size of ref 10 not stated (C27).** The chapter already says "a small study", which matches the abstract ("According to this small sample"). Adding the 10 models and two assessors is an editorial choice.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- B10 (finding 4): "Selective laser melting dominates" (Key Points) and "Powder bed fusion dominates" (11.2) are inconsistent. They are not; SLM is a powder bed fusion process (Table 11.1).
- A6 (finding 26): none of refs 5–11 addresses post-curing. Ref 9's abstract says properties are "affected by orientation, post-curing, and material choice".
- B5 (finding 12): Review Question 2 implies 210° was less accurate overall. The question asks only about deviation at the margin, which the source supports.
- B20 (finding 16): the legend lists contact areas that are not drawn. The legend states a general rule, matching the caption.
- B21 and C26 (finding 28): "Ref 13 (Herschdorfer)" is ambiguous. Herschdorfer is ref 13 in both the old and the new numbering.
- A18 (discarded 1): "Most are ceramic-filled methacrylates" is unsupported. See above.
- C18 (finding 1): "milling is not preferred because ref 23 found printed and milled comparable". The preference for milling rests on its clinical record (Chapter 10) and on ref 24, not on ref 23 alone; only the citation of in vitro reviews for "Clinical evidence" was kept.
