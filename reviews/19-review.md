## Discarded findings

1. **Uncited general statements (A20, B25 second part, C18 remaining items).** "Digital study models have replaced plaster in many orthodontic practices", "milled PMMA tolerates thinner sections than many printed resins", "CAD/CAM allows precise, reproducible designs" and "one of the most established uses of VSP". These are general descriptive statements with no reference attached and no factual error found. The PMMA statement is already listed as uncited general knowledge in the notes file. Ref 23's background ("The fibula free flap (FFF) remains the criterion standard for complex mandibular reconstruction") is consistent with the VSP statement; adding it is an editorial choice. ("Transformed" from C18 is kept as finding 17.)
2. **"Transfer accuracy", "linear accuracy", "maxillary vertical accuracy" against the trueness/precision rule (C19).** The cited studies themselves report these outcomes as accuracy (deviation from the plan), not as ISO 5725 scanner or fit data. The checker rated it borderline, and no error of fact is involved. If the author wants the rule extended beyond scanner and fit data, that belongs in STYLE.md.
3. **Ref 17 article number "8601" (B27).** PubMed gives volume 15, issue 21 and the PII "jcm15218601", which is consistent with 8601. The notes file already lists it for confirmation, so it is not a new error.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- B12 and C4 (finding 4): labelling ref 14 as a "laboratory" or in vitro study. The abstract says only that "The teeth were randomly assigned", without stating the setting, so the label cannot be confirmed.
- B13 (finding 28): "within about 0.3–0.7 mm [13,14]". Ref 14's abstract gives no deviation values; the range belongs to ref 13 only.
- B23 (finding 14): calling ref 11 "retrospective". The abstract does not use the word; the fix uses its own wording ("extracted from patient files").
- B24 (finding 19): that the smile design and wax-up as the source of the plan are not in ref 18. That clause is uncited description with a cross-reference to Chapter 6, and ref 18 says "The digital workflow defines the planned gingival margin and bone reduction level".
- B6 (finding 5): presenting the case types as clinical opinion is kept only as an option; the main fix removes them.

## Top items for the author

1. **#3 (major): Evidence Box "half of the planned movement per stage".** Ref 5 measured the whole treatment, not each stage, and ref 4 gives no "half" figure. Table 19.3 carries the same misattribution.
2. **#6 (major): Table 19.3 "low certainty [20,21]".** Ref 21 rated its certainty as high and reports no cost or radiation data. Cite ref 20 only.
3. **#4 (major): 19.4.1 "templates were lower in height" [14].** The abstract gives a significant height difference but not its direction. Reword, or mark [UNVERIFIED] until the full text is checked.
4. **#5 (major): VSP Evidence Box case types (asymmetry, multiple segments, syndromic, cleft).** Neither review lists them; both only recommend VSP for complex cases. "At higher cost" is from ref 20 only.
5. **#1 (major): 19.2.2 "The set-up is still a prediction".** This contradicts the Evidence Box, Key Points and Figure 19.1, which all say the set-up is a target, not a prediction. (Also note #2: the uncited "largest single CAD/CAM application by volume".)
