## Discarded findings

1. **6.2.5 and Table 6.1: "In a randomised study of 150 patients, fully automatic AI-based registration was as accurate as conventional methods" (A8, A9, B7, C8).** This is a false positive. The paper's own title calls it "A randomized clinical study", and its conclusion says AI registration "can be as accurate as conventional superimposition methods" without artefacts (38858787). The chapter follows the source's own labels.
2. **6.2.3: the list of artefact types is not in the ref 3 abstract (A20c, B23 first part, C20 part).** The list is not cited to ref 3 directly. The abstract says it reviews "the most prominent artefacts", and the listed types are standard textbook content. No error was found. A full-text check is optional.
3. **6.2.2 "The salivary and thyroid glands were among the largest contributors" is incomplete (C10).** The sentence is correct as written ("among the largest"). The abstract gives remainder tissues 37%, salivary glands 24% and thyroid 21%. Adding the percentages is an editorial choice.
4. **Uncited statements (A20a, b, d, e; B19; C20 other parts).** These cover the dose-justification Clinical Tip (ALARA), "Manual and semi-automatic segmentation is more accurate but slow", the strengths and limitations in Table 6.2, and the privacy statement in 6.6.3. No factual error was found. Ref 4 supports manual segmentation being more accurate and slower than AI. Refs 7 and 8 agree with Table 6.2 (structured light showed higher precision; smartphone systems lack resolution; stationary systems are expensive with a large footprint). The notes file already lists the Clinical Tip as uncited. Adding citations is an editorial choice.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- Finding 3 (A2, B13): the point that ref 9's precision (2.97 mm) lies above the range. The range describes trueness, and the fix labels it as trueness.
- Finding 6 (B8, C9): the change to Review Question 1 ("Which factor most strongly determines…"). The abstract singles out field size as the factor the dose is "strongly related" to, so the question can stand.
- Finding 11 (A17): the proposed wording "smartphone apps were the least accurate". Only the rear-camera app had the lowest accuracy in ref 7, so the fix names one app.
- Finding 19 (B23): the proposal to cite ref 4 for segmentation. Ref 4 reports no artefact effect (finding 1).
- Finding 22 (A21, B22, C21): the old reference and table numbers in the earlier notes sections ("[17]", "Refs 19 and 20", "Table 6.2"). The notes file states that those sections use the old numbering and gives the old-to-new mapping, including the table renumbering.
- Finding 22 (A21, B22): the claim that "UNVERIFIED items: 0 (listed below…)" contradicts itself. The section below says "None", which is consistent.
