# Chapter 16 review: Chairside CAD/CAM in Restorative Dentistry

**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Method:** Three independent checkers (A, B, C) each checked the chapter, its 21 references and the three figures. A false-positive filter then re-checked every merged finding against the chapter text, the PubMed abstract and metadata of the cited paper, and the SVG source for figure findings. Evidence is limited to PubMed abstracts and metadata; full texts were not read.

## Discarded findings

1. **Fig 16.3 lithium disilicate bar drawn short (B8).** This is a false positive. The checker read the `H700` value but missed the 4 px rounded end. The bar ends at x = 704, which is (704 − 330)/448 = 83.5%, as stated. The other bars end at 708, 727 and 722.
2. **Ref 18 "randomised trials and prospective studies" misattributed (C20).** The abstract describes a review that included 9 RCTs and 6 observational studies. The chapter sentence describes that scope correctly, and the abstract does not clearly separate which studies were used for the estimates.
3. **16.6.3 [UNVERIFIED] statement still needs a source (B17).** It is already marked [UNVERIFIED] as CLAUDE.md requires, and Checker C's PubMed search found no such trial. It is not a new error.
4. **16.1 "(and increasingly a printer)" not in ref 2 (B26, printer part).** This is not attached to ref 2. Chairside printing is supported later in the chapter (16.4.4, ref 12: "Additive chairside manufacturing of definitive hybrid composite SCs is now possible").
5. **Uncited material facts (A25, B23, C22, and the uncited parts of A10 and B21).** These cover the leucite, lithium disilicate and ZLS processing states; resin blocks that "mill quickly, need no firing, easy to repair"; deep margin elevation; and "printing is beginning to enter chairside practice". They are correct standard statements with no reference attached, and no factual error was found. The printing statement is supported by ref 12 in the same paragraph. Adding cross-references to Chapters 13 and 15 is an editorial choice.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- C11: the claim that the Fig 16.2 caption states a general rule from one study. The source's conclusion supports it ("Crystallization changes the relationship between the crown and tooth").
- B22 and C18: the point that ">850 MPa is manufacturer information". The chapter does not use that figure.
- C10: the point that the materials differ between the three workflows in ref 12. The chapter already names the material for each workflow and says the results are in vitro for one set of devices.

## Top items for the author

1. **#3 (major): Step 7, universal adhesives.** The chapter reverses the source. Universal adhesives did well on hybrid blocks. They did not match dedicated protocols for glass-ceramics and zirconia.
2. **#1 (major): 16.5.3 "larger restorations".** Ref 13 found that size had no influence. Ref 15 found the highest risk for three-surface restorations, not for the largest.
3. **#2 (major): 16.5.3 caries and debonding "usually linked to isolation and bonding quality".** Neither cited abstract supports the causal link. Ref 18 does not mention caries or debonding at all.
4. **#4 (major): bonding protocols in Step 7 and Key Points.** The evidence is mostly in vitro and needs a label. The source says definitive clinical recommendations cannot yet be made, so Key Points should not present it as a firm protocol.
5. **#5 (major): Key Points "Avoid chairside work…" cited to ref 4.** Ref 4, an in vitro scanning study, supports only the unscannable-margin item. Mark the other three as expert opinion.
