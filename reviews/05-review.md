**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Method:** No subagent tool was available in this session, so the three checks and the filter were run by one agent as four separate passes, in sequence: Pass A (citation existence, reference details, abstract support and exact numbers), Pass B (overstatement, evidence level, uncited claims), Pass C (internal consistency between text, tables, figures, Key Points, Review Questions and the notes file, plus the SVG data of Figures 5.1–5.3). A final filter pass re-checked every merged finding against the chapter text, the PubMed abstract and metadata of the cited paper (PubMed connector), and the SVG source. Because the passes were not independent, "Raised by" shows the pass(es) in which a finding came up, not independent agreement. Evidence is limited to PubMed abstracts and metadata; full texts were not read.

## Discarded findings

1. **Ref 12 author list incomplete.** False positive. PubMed lists 7 authors; the chapter gives the first 6 and "et al.", which is Vancouver style. The same applies to refs 3 and 9 (7 authors each) and ref 14.
2. **Figures 5.1 and 5.2 bars not to scale.** False positive. With 7.167 px per µm from x = 330, every bar (including its 4 px rounded end) ends at the stated value (see Summary).
3. **Ref 13 year 2025 versus 2023.** False positive. PubMed gives print 2025 Aug;134(2):450–459 and e-publication 11 Nov 2023. The chapter uses the print year, as the notes say.
4. **Ref 8 missing volume and pages.** False positive. PubMed has none (published 22 Sep 2026); the DOI is correct.
5. **5.7.3 "8.1° versus 2.1°" and "about 0.2–0.5° versus 1.3–1.9°".** False positive. These are correct roundings of 8.14° and 2.13° (37957064) and of 0.19–0.53° and 1.34–1.88° (37936837).
6. **5.5 "High-accuracy laboratory scans of implant casts are also the usual reference… [3,11]".** False positive. Both abstracts use a laboratory scan of the master cast as the reference (42349627, 41680062).
7. **5.5 "The fit and seating of the scan body matter as much as the scanner".** Not an error. Ref 10 shows reseating discrepancies (11 and 39 µm) of the same order as or larger than the digitising error (5 and 13 µm).
8. **Ref 5 does not support disinfecting casts (5.8 and Key Points).** Not confirmed as an error. The review (31467901) discusses disinfecting impressions and other items and calls gypsum casts "a hidden source of infection"; the advice follows from it.
9. **Table 5.1 rows on stitching and soft-tissue displacement are uncited.** These are standard technical statements with no reference attached, and no factual error was found.
10. **5.7.3 ref 14 "Ear-based facebows can transfer asymmetries of the ears into the mounting" goes beyond the source.** False positive. The study tested exactly this setting (3 mm ear height difference) and found the facebow less accurate in the frontal view (37936837).

## Top items for the author

1. **#1 (major): Table 5.1 "About 10 µm or better in clinical comparisons".** It rests on one patient and one scanner and conflicts with the 45.5 µm mean precision of six laboratory scanners in 5.3. Give both values with their context.
2. **#2 (major): laboratory scanners are "considerably more precise" than intraoral scanning.** The claim comes from one patient and one intraoral scanner from 2013, and the Key Points cite refs 2 and 3, which do not compare the two. State the sample.
3. **#14 (minor): 5.7.2 mounting approaches cited to ref 12.** The abstract supports only the virtual facebow. Confirm the other two from the full text or mark them [UNVERIFIED].
4. **#9 and #10 (minor): direct impression scanning evidence.** Ref 7 measured precision in 5 participants, and ref 6 does not mention "accessible preparations". Tighten the wording to match the abstracts.
5. **#5, #6, #11 and #13 (minor): missing in vitro labels** for refs 4, 2, 9 and 10, including Review Question 4's "implants in the mouth".
