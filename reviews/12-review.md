## Discarded findings

1. **12.2.1 "wide and growing range of zirconia types" [6] (A23).** The word "growing" is not a factual error: the review describes 15 years of new zirconia types (4Y-, 5Y-, multilayered) and says "the wide range of zirconia materials currently available in the market may cause confusion". The claim attached to [6] (follow manufacturers' instructions) is supported.
2. **12.7.1 "edge chipping depended on material type, milling surface and edge location" [2] (C10).** These were the experimental factors of a controlled in vitro study, and the abstract reports that they "significantly affected" or were "significantly associated" with chipping. "Depended on" is a fair summary here. The Key Point version, which adds "design" and "weakens the restoration", is kept as finding 26.

Sub-claims removed from findings that were otherwise confirmed:
- A18: "The final surface affects plaque retention…" in 12.4 is uncited. It is a standard background statement with no reference attached, and no factual error was found. Only the glaze-wear mechanism is kept (finding 13).
- C19: "Conventional sintering cycles for zirconia take many hours" is a standard, uncited statement with no error found. Only the "few micrometres" thickness is kept (finding 9).
- C17: adding [14] to the 12.5.1 opening sentence. In [14], "Phase transformation was occurred in all groups", including the glazed-only control, so it does not support a grinding-specific transformation. Only [13] supports it (finding 14).
- A19 and C22: the Fig 12.1 note "Fit changes during firing: check fit after it". The figure is a generic schematic, and Table 12.1 already states that zirconia shrinks to final size on sintering. Only the Lab Note is kept (finding 8).

## Top items for the author

1. **#1–#3 (major): re-glazing ground zirconia.** In both cited studies, the strength drop after re-glazing was relative to ground-only specimens. In [13] re-glazed zirconia was as strong as the unground control; in [14] polished and re-glazed groups did not differ at all, and only the authors' conclusion says otherwise. The same claim appears in 12.5.1, step 5, Fig 12.2, Table 12.2 and Key Points, which also cite [12], [15] and [16] for it without support.
2. **#4 (major): Table 12.3 "material-specific sequence [9,10,15]".** Ref 10 found that the zirconia kit gave the lowest roughness on every ceramic tested, so it does not support material-specific polishing.
3. **#11 and #25: polishing kits and Key Point 4.** Only one of four kits matched glaze in [9], and only ref 8 tested strength. Key Point 4 should cite strength to [8] alone.
4. **#9 and #13: uncited statements presented as fact.** The "few micrometres" stain-layer thickness and the glaze-wear mechanism in 12.4.1 have no source.
5. **#26: Key Point on edge chipping.** Neither study tested design, and ref 2 reports a correlation in milled bars, not that chipping "weakens the restoration".
