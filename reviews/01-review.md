## Discarded findings

1. **1.8.2 "short-span FDPs" attributed to Mangano [8].** The abstract says "fixed partial dentures" without "short-span". But in the same abstract the literature "does not support the use of IOS in long-span restorations", so "short-span" is a fair reading. It is not an error.
2. **Key Points "about 84–89%… at 16–18 years".** This is consistent with the abstracts: 84.4% at 16.7 y with no further event up to 18.3 y [16], and 88.7% at 17 y [17]. Reiss's 89% "restoration still in place" figure is also within the range.
3. **Ref 22 cited as 2020 although published online in 2019.** Vancouver uses the print year (PubMed: Jan 2020; 36(1):9–24). This is not an error.
4. **1.2.2 "the long time needed to make ceramic restorations in the laboratory was a major barrier" [2].** This is a fair paraphrase of "raising the issue of the fast fabrication of the ceramic restorations" together with the single-appointment rationale.
5. **1.4.2 "printing of ceramics for definitive restorations was not yet clinically established [9]".** This is supported by 26935333: "The 3D printing technology… does not include ceramics and is limited to polymers."
6. **Ref 2 supplement page range doubtful (notes file).** The metadata confirm "137 Suppl, 7S-13S". This is not an error (the notes item is closed in finding 25).
7. **Uncited general statements** (1.1 aerospace and automotive use; 1.2.3 "Europe, North America and Japan"; 1.2.4 "Since about 2010" list; 1.3.2 "AI-assisted design proposals are becoming common"; Table 1.1 2020s row; 1.6 and 1.10; 1.8.3 "since been replaced by faster, powder-free devices"). None has a reference attached, no factual error was found, and the notes file already lists 1.6 and 1.10 as uncited general knowledge. As in the Chapter 16 review, adding sources or cross-references to Chapters 4, 7 and 20 is an editorial choice. The AI-assisted design sentence is the one most worth a source or a cross-reference to Chapter 7.
