# Chapter 02 review: The Digital Workflow and Data Formats

**Date:** 2026-11-22
**Method:** The subagent (Agent) tool was not available in this session, so the three independent checks were run as three separate passes by one reviewer. Pass A checked citation existence and reference details. Pass B checked each cited claim and every number against the PubMed abstract, and looked for overstatement. Pass C checked uncited claims, internal consistency between text, tables, Key Points and review questions, and the SVG source of the three figures. A separate false-positive filter pass then re-read every merged finding against the chapter text, the PubMed abstract and metadata, and the SVG source, and discarded the findings it could not confirm. Evidence is limited to PubMed abstracts and metadata (PubMed connector); full texts were not read.

## Discarded findings

1. **Table 2.2 file formats uncited (C).** The notes file already lists this as general technical knowledge. The content was checked and is correct: STL carries no units or colour, PLY stores per-vertex colour, OBJ uses separate texture and material files, and 3MF holds units, materials and multiple parts.
2. **Figure 2.1 has no "Bite and articulation" row, unlike Table 2.1 (C).** The figure shows a subset of the steps. Nothing contradicts the table.
3. **Ref 19 called a "randomised clinical study" (B).** This follows the title of the paper. The abstract states "150 randomly selected patients", and the chapter does not present it as an RCT of treatments.
4. **Ref 13 "first published proof-of-principle" (B).** This is supported: "the first approach that realized the fusion of intraoral and facial surfaces combined with skeletal anatomy imaging". Only the year is a problem (kept as finding 18).
5. **Ref 1 "less than half the time" (B).** This matches "more than twofold faster (75.3 min) … (156.6 min)".
6. **Ref 7 "A digital file cannot carry infection" (B).** The abstract supports it ("digital impression and casts are not a source of cross-infection"), and the statement is true.
7. **2.8.2 "this error is much larger than scanner error" (B).** This compares across studies, but the numbers support it (0.50–1.64 mm trueness [18] against tens of µm in ref 3: 10–50 µm mean deviations).
8. **Ref 15 "Two-dimensional photographs and smartphone videos" (B).** The abstract mentions photographs and "smartphone videos … 2D smile frame … 3D digital smile design".
9. **Ref 9 "Moderate reduction is safe for large anatomical models" (B).** This is supported by "can be reduced up to 50%, with neglectable concern to inaccuracy".
10. **Figure 2.3 scanner bar width (C).** It is drawn to scale (about 60 µm) and agrees with "tens of µm". The notes file already records that this value is illustrative.
11. **Ref 12 "limited by data availability, data structure and methodological standards" (B).** This matches the abstract: "limited data availability, accessibility, structure … lacking methodological rigor and standards".
12. **Ref 3 "and lower again than a desktop laboratory scanner" wording (B).** This is a style point and not a factual error. The values (50, 25 and 10 µm) and their order are correct. The sentence is reworded anyway in finding 7.

## Top items for the author

1. **#1 (major): 2.2.2 cost saving.** The "18% lower total treatment costs" is the cost-minimisation figure. Direct treatment costs fell by about 14% (1815 vs 2120 CHF), and the two arms also used different abutments and crowns.
2. **#2 (major): Clinical Tip "well supported".** The systematic review cited just above found only three RCTs, none with follow-up, and said it could give no recommendations for clinical routine.
3. **#3 (major): Key Points "hole-filling affect clinical accuracy [8,9]".** Neither study is clinical: one is on typodonts and one on cadaver CBCT models. Neither looks at hole-filling.
4. **#17 and #14–16: data-protection wording.** The quotation should read "front and center" and include "rights". The points about GDPR special categories, ISO 27001 and HIPAA business associates are not in the cited source and need a primary legal source or an [UNVERIFIED] mark.
5. **#4, #21, #23: give study sizes.** The evidence on complete arches (10 patients), the virtual patient (10 participants) and patient preference and cost (20 patients each) is small. The text and Key Points currently read as general findings.
