## Weak evidence
- Most mechanical data are **in vitro**, single-study values (Refs 6, 10, 18). Flexural strengths differ widely between studies for the same material class. For example, ZLS was ~444 MPa in Elsaka [18] but factory-crystallised lithium silicates were ~172–202 MPa in Vichi [10]. The text presents this as a conflict on purpose. It does not average the values.
- **Antagonist wear:** two meta-analyses conflict (Refs 25 and 26). Ref 26 is in *Dentistry Journal* (MDPI) and Ref 25 in *J Clin Med* (MDPI). A Shah 2024 umbrella review (*J Indian Prosthodont Soc*), which favours polished zirconia, was found but not cited.
- The ZLS clinical evidence comes from one group's studies (Rinke et al., Refs 20 and 21) plus one 12-month implant RCT (Ref 22). The data are short to medium term.
- The self-etching primer meta-analysis (Ref 24) covers in vitro studies only. The authors say explicitly that it does not show clinical equivalence.
- *Materials* (MDPI) and *J Clin Exp Dent* are lower-tier journals (Refs 6, 10, 13).

## Caveats / uncited statements
- **Lithium disilicate processing (13.4.1):** the bluish, partially crystallised state milled before a crystallisation firing, and the needle-like final crystals, are standard manufacturer and textbook knowledge. They have no citation. Exact temperatures were left out on purpose.
- **Leucite crystals in a glassy matrix improving crack resistance (13.3.1),** and feldspathic blocks being "mostly glassy" (13.2.1): textbook statements, uncited.
- **Etching protocol principle (13.7.2):** feldspathic/leucite ceramics need longer etching than lithium disilicate and lithium silicates. This is standard guidance, uncited. Specific concentrations and times were left out; the text points readers to the manufacturer.
- **Table 13.2:** "Conventional cements (e.g., glass-ionomer) are not generally recommended for glass-ceramics" is uncited expert guidance.
- **HF toxicity safety tip:** Tian 2014 [5] supports HF toxicity in general. The specific safety advice is clinical opinion.
- **Section 13.8.2 and Key Points:** "Well-polished surfaces wear opposing teeth less" is now labelled expert opinion in the text; the cited meta-analyses [25,26] do not test surface finish.
- **Section 13.1.2:** the selective dissolution of the glassy phase by HF, exposing silica for silane, is textbook mechanism, uncited. Only the statement that HF plus silane is the most widely accepted treatment is cited [5].
- **Expert-opinion labels (review 2026-11-22):** leucite/feldspathic indications (Key Points), lithium disilicate preferred for posterior crowns (13.3.2), and leucite "posterior crowns under high load" (Table 13.1).
- **Section 13.1.2:** the claim that CAD/CAM blocks are more homogeneous than hand-layered porcelain refers back to Chapter 1 (Beuer 2008).
