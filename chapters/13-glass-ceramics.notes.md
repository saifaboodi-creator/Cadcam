## Weak evidence
- Most mechanical data are **in vitro**, single-study values (Refs 5, 9, 16). Flexural strengths differ widely between studies for the same material class. For example, ZLS was ~444 MPa in Elsaka [16] but factory-crystallised lithium silicates were ~172–202 MPa in Vichi [9]. The text presents this as a conflict on purpose. It does not average the values.
- **Antagonist wear:** two meta-analyses conflict (Refs 24 and 25). Ref 25 is in *Dentistry Journal* (MDPI) and Ref 24 in *J Clin Med* (MDPI). A Shah 2024 umbrella review (*J Indian Prosthodont Soc*), which favours polished zirconia, was found but not cited.
- The ZLS clinical evidence comes from one group's studies (Rinke et al., Refs 18 and 19) plus one 12-month implant RCT (Ref 20). The data are short to medium term.
- The self-etching primer meta-analysis (Ref 23) covers in vitro studies only. The authors say explicitly that it does not show clinical equivalence.
- *Materials* (MDPI) and *J Clin Exp Dent* are lower-tier journals (Refs 5, 9, 11).

## Caveats / uncited statements
- **Lithium disilicate processing (13.4.1):** the bluish, partially crystallised state milled before a crystallisation firing, and the needle-like final crystals, are standard manufacturer and textbook knowledge. They have no citation. Exact temperatures were left out on purpose.
- **Leucite crystals in a glassy matrix improving crack resistance (13.3.1),** and feldspathic blocks being "mostly glassy" (13.2.1): textbook statements, uncited.
- **Etching protocol principle (13.7.2):** feldspathic/leucite ceramics need longer etching than lithium disilicate and lithium silicates. This is standard guidance, uncited. Specific concentrations and times were left out; the text points readers to the manufacturer.
- **Table 13.2:** "Conventional cements (e.g., glass-ionomer) are not generally recommended for glass-ceramics" is uncited expert guidance.
- **HF toxicity safety tip:** Tian 2014 [22] supports HF toxicity in general. The specific safety advice is clinical opinion.
- **Section 13.8.2:** "Well-polished surfaces wear opposing teeth less" is described as a practical message drawn from the literature. The cited meta-analyses do not test it directly.
- **Section 13.1.2:** the claim that CAD/CAM blocks are more homogeneous than hand-layered porcelain refers back to Chapter 1 (Beuer 2008).
