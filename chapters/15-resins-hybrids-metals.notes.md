## Weak evidence
- **Interim materials (refs 13, 14):** single-centre in vitro fracture tests. They show the order of materials, not clinical survival. Ref 14 is mainly a PLA study that also included milled PMMA.
- **PEEK and PEKK (refs 16–20):** mostly in vitro, low certainty. The authors of ref 18 call their findings "hypothesis generating". RPD clinical studies are sparse (ref 19 includes 4 clinical studies). Refs 19 and 20 partly conflict: ref 19 found similar retention, ref 20 found lower retention and fatigue resistance for PEEK. The text presents both.
- **Co-Cr fit (refs 21–23):** all in vitro, and they disagree on the ranking of SLM and milling. The text concludes that all routes are acceptable and the result depends on the process and alloy, rather than ranking them.
- **Implant PICN (refs 9–11):** strong RCT signal, but ref 9's authors limit their conclusion to their own chairside, perforated-block, Ti-base set-up. Ref 11 is exploratory and not powered for equivalence.
- **Ref 5 (Polat 2024):** practice-based series, short mean follow-up (18.3 months).
- **Ref 6 (Flueras 2026):** qualitative systematic review of mostly in vitro studies.
- **Ref 15:** a systematic review with few clinical studies and mixed designs.

## Caveats and uncited statements
- Table 15.1, "Typical bonding" row: "air abrasion or as instructed" for dispersed-filler composites and "HF etching and silane, or as instructed" for PICN is general manufacturer practice and has no citation. Confirm it against current instructions for use.
- 15.4.1: the general properties of PAEK polymers (light, tough, low modulus close to bone and dentine, opaque or greyish, need veneering) are uncited textbook knowledge. The statement that a "shock-absorbing" benefit lacks clinical proof is an editorial judgement.
- 15.5.1: the four Co-Cr manufacturing routes are a general description. The routes themselves appear in refs 21–23.
- 15.5.3: titanium "difficult to cast accurately" and milling being the "standard route" for titanium frameworks are general knowledge. Ref 25 supports the shift to CAD/CAM milled titanium.
- 15.5.2: "hard milling … slowest and wears tools" is general manufacturing knowledge with no citation.
- Lab Note in 15.4.5 (PEEK RPDs need bulkier connectors and clasps) is an inference from the lower rigidity reported in ref 20 and general design principles. It is not a direct finding.
- Table 15.4 is a synthesis for teaching. Rows that point to Chapters 9, 11, 13 and 14 depend on those chapters.

## Decisions made
- Treated resin-matrix ceramics (PICN plus resin nanoceramics) together for clinical outcomes, following ref 3. Kept the microstructural distinction from ref 1.
- Gave the implant-PICN evidence its own warning section (15.2.5) with an Evidence Box. RCT evidence and the S3 guideline agree, and the contrast with good results on natural teeth is teaching-relevant.
- Did not use brand names in the text. They appear only in reference titles (for example "Cerec" in ref 9), following STYLE.md.
- Kept 3D-printed resins and laser melting at overview level and cross-referred to Chapter 11 to avoid overlap. Cross-referred Chapter 18 for RPD design and Chapter 9 for Ti-bases and frameworks.
- Did not give numeric property values (flexural strength, modulus) for individual materials. Only the relative statements in ref 1 could be verified, and product values vary.
